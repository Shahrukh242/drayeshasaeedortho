Screen Time and Bone Health: The Impact of Devices on Children's Spines

By Dr. Ayesha Saeed • May 25, 2026 • 5 mins read

With children spending more time on tablets, smartphones, and computers, pediatric orthopedists are seeing a surge in back pain, rounded shoulders, and 'tech neck.' Excessive screen time impact goes beyond eyes and attention; it affects bone density and spine health.

Understanding 'Tech Neck' in Children

When a child looks down at a tablet or phone, their head juts forward. This posture dramatically increases the effective weight of the head on the neck muscles and cervical spine. Over time, this causes muscle strain, disc pressure, and can lead to a permanent slouched posture or kyphosis.

The Threat of a Sedentary Lifestyle

Childhood is the critical window for building peak bone mass. Bones strengthen in response to weight-bearing physical activity (running, jumping, playing). High screen time leads to a sedentary lifestyle, resulting in lower bone mineral density, weaker muscles, and higher risk of fractures.

Tips to Protect Your Child's Bones and Spine

  • Limit Screen Time: Follow age-appropriate screen limits. Encourage at least 60 minutes of active outdoor play daily.
  • Use Ergonomic Screen Positions: Raise screens to eye level to avoid looking down. Remind children to sit back in a chair with foot support instead of slouching on a sofa.
  • Follow the 20-20-20 Rule: Every 20 minutes, have your child stand up, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds, and stretch their neck and back.
