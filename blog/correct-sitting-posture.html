Correct Sitting Posture for Children: The Dangers of W-Sitting

By Dr. Ayesha Saeed • June 5, 2026 • 4 mins read

How your child sits on the floor during play can have a significant impact on their physical development. One posture that often raises concern among pediatric orthopedists is 'W-sitting.' Here is why you should discourage this habit and how to promote healthy posture.

What is W-Sitting?

W-sitting is a position where a child sits on their bottom with their knees bent and their feet flared out to the sides, forming a 'W' shape when viewed from above. Children often choose this position because it provides a wide, stable base, making it easy to play without using core muscles to balance.

Why W-Sitting Can Be Harmful

While temporary W-sitting is common, making it a habit puts excessive strain on developing joints and muscles:

  • Hip Instability: W-sitting places the hip joints in extreme internal rotation, increasing the risk of hip subluxation, particularly in children with developmental hip issues.
  • Muscle Tightness: It keeps the hamstring and calf muscles shortened, leading to muscle tightness and toe-walking.
  • Weak Core Development: Because the position supports the child's body weight, they do not develop active core and back muscles needed for good standing posture.

Healthy Alternative Postures

Gently encourage your child to sit in alternative positions: cross-legged (Indian style), side-sitting (legs bent to one side), or long-sitting (legs straight out in front). If a child cannot sit comfortably in these positions or complains of hip pain, consult a specialist.
